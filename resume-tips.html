Actionable advice from recruiting veterans and ATS engineers to help you win more job interviews.

Fundamentals

How to Make a Professional Resume

A professional resume serves as a high-density, targeted marketing document. Its singular goal is not to catalog every chore you have ever performed, but to prove you possess the skills and track record necessary to solve the employer’s current problems.

Core Sections Every Professional Resume Requires:

  • Contact Header: Name, professional headline, phone, email, location (City, State/Country), and LinkedIn profile URL.
  • Professional Summary: 3 to 4 sentences highlighting years of experience, core hard skills, and an overarching career accomplishment.
  • Work Experience: Reverse-chronological history detailing employer, role, dates, and bullet points containing measurable outcomes.
  • Education: Academic credentials, relevant major coursework, certifications, and academic distinctions.
  • Skills: Grouped into technical proficiencies, specialized software, and transferable competencies.
ATS Algorithm Secrets

What is an ATS-Friendly Resume?

Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) are software applications (such as Workday, Taleo, Greenhouse, iCIMS, and Lever) used by employers to collect, parse, rank, and organize thousands of incoming applications.

The Golden Rule of ATS Compliance:

Never sacrifice machine readability for artistic decoration. A human recruiter can only admire your resume if the software parser parses it first!

Why Resumes Fail ATS Parsing:

  • Placing contact info inside image headers or footers where bots cannot read text.
  • Using non-standard section headers like "My Journey" instead of "Work Experience".
  • Complex multi-column tables that cause the parser to read across columns horizontally, scrambling text.
  • Submitting image-based PDFs or flat JPEGs instead of text-searchable vector PDFs.
Impact Writing

How to Write a Resume Summary (The Formula)

Avoid vague declarations like "Hardworking professional seeking opportunity". Instead, adopt our proven 3-part formula:

Sentence 1 (Identity & Scope):

[Job Title] with [X] years of experience specializing in [Core Competency 1] and [Core Competency 2].

Sentence 2 (Measurable Value):

Track record of [Quantifiable Achievement, e.g., maintaining 99.8% compliance audit score or improving process speed by 25%].

Sentence 3 (Target Alignment):

Dedicated to leveraging expertise in [Key Tool / Methodology] to drive operational excellence for [Target Employer].

Entry-Level

How to Create a Resume for a Fresh Graduate

If you have little or no formal work history, you possess more transferable proof than you realize. Recruiters evaluate fresh graduates based on potential, diligence, and foundational competence.

What to Feature Ahead of Work History:

  • Academic Projects: Capstone projects, research papers, lab assignments, or software projects with practical applications.
  • Leadership & Clubs: Roles in student societies, hackathons, volunteer committees, or athletic teams.
  • Technical Stack & Certifications: Industry certifications (ASIS, CompTIA, AWS, Google) that prove self-motivated learning.
  • Relevant Coursework: High-level advanced electives that match the requirements in the job description.
Recruiter Secret

Writing Experience Bullets: The Google "X-Y-Z" Formula

Former Google SVP of People Operations Laszlo Bock shared the most effective formula for resume bullet points:

"Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y], by doing [Z]"

❌ Weak Responsibility Bullet:

"Responsible for watching CCTV camera feeds and writing logs."

✓ High-Impact X-Y-Z Bullet:

"Monitored 120+ PTZ video streams, reducing security incident dispatch lag by 35% through proactive Milestone VMS threat detection."
